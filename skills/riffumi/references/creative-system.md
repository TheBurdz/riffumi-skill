# Creative system: concepts, structures, devices, kits

A **format** (a 15 s vertical ad, a 60 s listing video) is a container. A **look** (fonts,
colours, transitions) is a coat of paint. Neither is an idea. Template-looking videos come
from starting with the container and the paint. Start with the idea.

## The concept = idea + structure + device

- **Idea**: one sentence a stranger would repeat. "The lights are the party, not the
  decoration." "Every step is one tap." "Twelve years in one minute."
- **Structure**: the order in which the viewer learns things (below). It decides the beats.
- **Signature device**: one memorable move the whole piece is built around (below). It is
  what makes this video recognisably this video. One per piece; do not stack three.

Then the **kit**: type, colour, grade, motion vocabulary, sound palette - all chosen to serve
the idea, from `motion-and-type.md`.

### Writing three concepts that are actually different

1. List the subject's facts and the one feeling the viewer should leave with.
2. Pick three **different structures** (not three variations of one). Mix families: one
   rational (demonstration, three reasons, how-to), one emotional (day with it, origin story,
   memory), one playful or surprising (exaggeration, repeat-repeat-twist, two worlds).
3. Give each a device that fits its structure (the pairings below are starting points).
4. Name each with a working title and pitch it in two lines: the idea, then what the viewer
   sees in the first two seconds and how it ends.

Kill a concept that could be pasted onto a competitor's product unchanged.

### Score them, then build the winner

| Criterion | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Surprise | the obvious feature list | a turn the viewer did not see coming |
| Specificity | could be any product | only works for this subject (its colours, use, words, quirks) |
| Visual payoff | text over photos | a shot people would screenshot (reveal, transformation, device) |
| Energy curve | flat | builds, drops, breathes, lands on the last hit |

Build the highest total. If none scores 14+, write new ones; the first idea is usually the
safest one, not the best.

### Plain vs produced - the same facts, two treatments

Product: a 100 ft colour-changing LED strip with an app, a remote and music sync.

**Plain (score 7):** "Show the product, list the features, end with a buy button." Five product
photos with captions: "100 FT" / "16 million colors" / "App control" / "Music sync" / "Buy now".

**Produced (score 18):** *"Your room called."* Structure: tease and reveal into three reasons.
Device: the room changes colour on every beat. Frame 0: a dark bedroom (b-roll) with the line
"YOUR ROOM CALLED." already on screen; on the drop the lights flood colour (beat-flash over
neon footage), "16M COLORS" lands word by word; the product pops in as a glowing sticker with its
real 4.4-star rating; a three-way split of people dancing, gaming and relaxing in coloured light;
"IT DANCES TO YOUR MUSIC" on the beat; the app in a phone; the end card on the last hit.

What changed: an idea a person would repeat, footage of the world the product lives in, the music
driving the cuts, every fact turned into a moment, and one device carried through.

### Make every act escalate

- Act 1 (hook, 0-15%): one line, moving footage, the question or the promise. Calm or tense.
- Act 2 (build, 15-70%): the drop. Shorter scenes, kinetic type, b-roll on the beat, proof graphics.
- Act 3 (payoff, 70-90%): the longest hold - the reveal, the result, the transformation.
- Close (90-100%): brand and one call to action, on the final hit, held 2-4 s.

Each act should feel bigger than the last: faster cuts, bolder type, one more layer of motion.


## Creative levels

| Level | What it means |
|---|---|
| safe | Brand-locked, calm: the brand's fonts and colours, dissolves and hard cuts, little motion. |
| bold (default) | A distinct kit and one signature device, still inside every platform rule. |
| wild | Display type, effects, punch zooms, speed contrast, fast transitions, overlays. Still truthful and still inside platform policy. |

When making several videos for one person or one batch, give each its own structure, device
and kit. Keep a list of what you used and exclude it next time.

Shares are % of running time. "Close" = name, offer, call to action or dedication.

Rules that ride along: comparisons on marketplaces stay within the same brand; how-to and
demonstration need real footage of the thing being done (stills alone cannot prove action);
memory and tribute pieces avoid marketing devices and UI pops.

## Signature devices

How to build each with `render.py` (or any editor). Use one; make it visible early and pay it off.

**Openings**
- **Question open** - frame 0 is a question in the viewer's words, full screen, no picture fight; the answer is the first cut.
- **Text-only open** - 1-2 s of type on a colour or gradient before any picture.
- **Word-by-word hook** - the hook line builds one word per beat (`letters`/`stagger` entrance, or one text layer per word with staggered `start`).
- **Macro open** - start on an extreme detail (crop or `camera` keyframes at zoom 2-3), pull out to the whole.
- **Noir open** - first beats in `noir`/`mono`, colour arrives with the product (`grade_to` or a `flash` transition).
- **Problem flash** - three 0.3 s cuts of the irritation, each with one word, then a hard stop.
- **Voice first** - the voice starts over black or an abstract shot; the picture arrives on the key word.

**Rhythm and time**
- **On the beat** - every cut on a beat (`"beats": N`), text pops on beats, `beat-flash` on the energetic stretch.
- **Quick-cut montage** - 4-8 cuts of 1-2 beats each, then a long hold.
- **Hold and release** - a long still hold with rising music, released into a fast run.
- **Speed contrast** - real-time, then 4-8x fast-forward through the boring part (`speed`), then real-time.
- **Speed-ramp reveal** - `speed` 2-3 ramping to 0.4 (`speed_to`) as the product enters.
- **Freeze-frame label** - `freeze` at the key frame + a callout block or label that names what you see.
- **Silence beat** - music drops out for 0.5-1 s before the reveal (split the music or duck it with a gap).
- **Countdown** - a counter or numbers ticking to the reveal or the offer end (only real deadlines).
- **Loop seam** - last frame matches the first so the video loops invisibly (same shot, same framing).

**Space and framing**
- **Pull-out reveal** - start tight (zoom 2+) and pull to the whole over the hold.
- **Depth pull** - slow push on a still with `soft-focus` or a cutout layer over a blurred copy for depth.
- **Detail trio** - three tight crops of one photo or three macro shots, then the whole.
- **Split screen** - two image/clip layers side by side or top/bottom, same timing, different outcomes.
- **Split benefit panels** - the frame divided into 2-3 panels, each a benefit, filling in sequence.
- **Horizon match** - consecutive shots aligned on the same horizon line or shape.
- **Match-cut chain** - each cut matches the previous shot's shape, colour or motion direction.
- **Object portal** - push into an object (a screen, a window, a box) and cut out of a matching shape in the next scene (`circle-open`, `zoom`).
- **POV handheld** - `handheld` camera + first-person framing and text ("POV: ...").

**Type as design**
- **Kinetic word stack** - benefit words stack and lock into a block, each landing on a beat.
- **Text behind subject** - a giant word behind the product or person (`behind_subject` on an image scene, or cutout layer over text).
- **Captions as design** - big word-timed captions are the main graphic (`captions` layer with highlight).
- **Typewriter line** - one line typed out, cursor feel, monospace or editorial serif.
- **Tracked tagline** - the closing line expands from tight to wide tracking (`tracking`).
- **Chapter cards** - a numbered or titled card before each section.
- **Lower-third story** - names, places, dates in lower thirds carry the narrative.
- **Quote lines** - one line of a quote per hold, the author last.
- **Place-name kinetic** - the place name assembles letter by letter over the defining view.
- **Date stamp** - a camcorder-style date or time in a corner, changing each scene.
- **Final still tagline** - end on the best still, held, one line, nothing else.
- **Slow reveal title** - the title fades or blurs in over 1.5-2 s while the camera drifts.

**Colour and light**
- **Mono to colour** - before in `mono`, after in full colour (`grade_to`, or a hard cut).
- **Grade flip** - the problem in `cool`/`muted`, the solution `warm`/`vivid`.
- **Grade journey** - colour travels across the piece (cool morning to warm evening).
- **Light-sweep climax** - a `light-sweep` overlay crosses the hero frame at the peak.
- **Accent wipe** - a brand-colour bar wipes across between benefits (`shape` + `draw`, or `wipe` transitions).
- **Colour-block cards** - flat colour scenes (one per point) with big type, alternating colours.
- **Weather arc** / **sky climax** - mood overlays build (dust -> sun rays -> light leak) toward the view.
- **Living still** - a still made to breathe: slow push + light leak or dust overlay + grain.

**Product and proof**
- **Hands first** - every shot starts with hands entering; the product is always in use.
- **Cutout pedestal** - the product cut out, floating with a contact shadow over a coloured stage (`card` with `cutout`, `float`).
- **Sticker slam** - cutout with white outline slams in (`pop`/`elastic`, slight rotation) on the beat.
- **Number counter** - the key number counts up (`counter` block) - only real numbers.
- **Before/after swipe** - one image wipes into the other (`wipe` transition over matched framing).
- **Then and now** - an old photo or state next to the new, labelled with years.
- **Hidden announcement** - a normal-looking post or scene that turns out to announce the launch.
- **Pattern-interrupt zoom** - a sudden punch zoom + `shake` + sound hit at the moment of surprise.
- **Crash cut** - loud to silent (or the reverse) on a hard cut, mid-motion.
- **Bookend** / **end-card echo** - the last frame repeats the first with one thing changed.
- **Close sound** - foley-level sound of the product (click, pour, zip) carries the edit (`audio` on the clip).

## Kit (the look)

Pick each from the concept, then check they agree:

- **Headline type** + **support type** (contrast in weight or class: condensed display + clean sans, editorial serif + mono, handwriting + geometric sans).
- **Palette**: 1 dominant, 1 accent, neutrals - from the brand or the footage itself (sample the product colour).
- **Grade**: one, optionally travelling.
- **Motion vocabulary**: 2-3 entrances, 1-2 camera moves, mostly hard cuts plus 1-2 accent transitions.
- **Texture** (optional): grain, light leaks, scanlines, confetti - only if it serves the world.
- **Sound palette**: music mood, sfx family (cinematic airs and risers; interface pops and clicks; comic boings) or none.
- **End card**: how the name, offer and CTA land.

## Anti-template rules

- No fixed scene list. The structure decides the beats; the material decides the shots.
- Moving footage first. A cut of stills and text cards is the template look; b-roll per beat
  (stock or generated) is the default unless the user's footage or the subject rules it out.
- Never the same kit twice in a batch, never the same opening twice for one person.
- Use the subject's own colours, shapes and words; quote its real details.
- Vary hold lengths; the key moment holds longest.
- One signature device, used with intent - not effect soup.
- If a stranger could not tell which product or person this is with the name covered, it is not done.
