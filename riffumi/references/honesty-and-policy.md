# Honesty, rights and policy

A video is an advertisement, a record or a memory. Each kind has rules that keep it trustworthy.

## Claims and facts
- On-screen claims, numbers, ratings, review counts, prices, offers, deadlines, awards and
  certifications come only from the user or a source you read (their page, their documents).
  Keep the list in `meta.facts`; `preflight.py` flags numbers that are not in it.
- Round only downward and say so ("42,000+" for 42,242). Do not upgrade "4.4" to "4.5".
- No superlatives you cannot support ("#1", "best", "clinically proven") unless the user has the
  evidence and the platform allows it.
- Prices and offers date quickly; avoid them in evergreen pieces (listing videos, website loops).
- Health, finance, kids' products and regulated categories have extra rules - keep claims to what
  the label and the user's documentation say.

## People, reviews and endorsements
- Only real reviews and quotes, attributed as the user supplies them; never write a review or a
  testimonial yourself and present it as a customer's.
- Stock people are models, not customers: do not caption them as reviewers, staff or users.
- No real person's likeness or voice (including AI imitations) without their permission.
- Do not build fake social posts, fake comments or fake notifications that pretend to be real;
  the platform-style blocks are for content the user actually received (or clearly fictional
  framing the user asked for).

## Comparisons
- Marketplaces generally forbid competitor names, logos and comparisons in listing and ad video;
  compare within the brand's own lineup or against "before".
- Elsewhere, comparisons must be fair, current and checkable.

## Intellectual property
- No copyrighted music, film clips, TV stills, artwork, characters, sports footage, or third-party
  logos and trademarks without rights. A stock clip that shows a branded product is not usable.
- Fonts: open-licence or user-supplied with rights.
- Respect stock and music licences (credits where required, no redistribution of raw files).

## AI disclosure
- Many ad platforms and social apps require labelling realistic AI-generated or altered people
  and scenes. Ask where the video will run and add the platform's AI label or a small on-screen
  note when needed.
- Generated imagery must never misrepresent what the product is, does or includes.

## Platform creative policies (examples - check the current rules)
- Ads: no misleading buttons or fake system UI, no excessive flashing/strobing, readable text,
  captions for muted autoplay, landing promise matches the ad.
- Marketplace listing video: no external links or contact details, no time-sensitive offers,
  no competitor references, product shown accurately.
- Photosensitivity: avoid more than 3 full-frame flashes per second (`beat-flash` and `flash`
  transitions on fast tempos can cross this - keep amounts moderate or skip on ads).

## Privacy
- People in the user's photos (guests, children, customers) appear only when the user is
  entitled to publish them; suggest blurring or cropping bystanders for public posts.
- Do not put personal data (addresses, phone numbers, plates) on screen unless the user asks and
  it is theirs to share.
